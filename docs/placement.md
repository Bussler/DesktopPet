# Placement

Hood uses display work areas, which exclude reserved desktop space such as the taskbar. Placement calculations use logical coordinates and each display's effective scale. The first area starts near the primary work area's bottom edge; its dimensions are at least large enough for the packaged pet and supported animation footprint. A smaller display can shrink the area to that minimum.

Dragging begins only after the system drag threshold. Hood and the area move together, and the same input is not also dispatched as a click. The destination is selected from Hood's position; the pet and area are clamped together at work-area edges. A valid drag is saved on release. If a saved monitor is disconnected, Hood is placed on the primary display. Display layout changes are reconciled while Hood is running.

Pointer movement continues to accumulate while placement is clamped, so a slow drag can cross a monitor boundary. On displays with different scaling, Hood and the area outline are positioned using the destination work area's physical origin and scale. Hood retains his position within the area, so the area can reach the screen edge while Hood himself remains inset.

Choose **Edit roaming area** from the pet menu to reveal the outline. Drag its top bar to move the area; Hood stays inside and moves only when the area would otherwise leave him outside. Drag any edge to resize that side, or any corner to resize both adjoining sides. The opposite edges stay fixed. Hover over the outline to see the resize cursor; corner handles are highlighted. Hood stays inside when the area shrinks, and resizing stops at his minimum footprint and the monitor's work-area edges. The editor is temporary, suspends ambient behavior, and saves successful changes in the same session file.

Hood can roam horizontally, vertically, or diagonally within the area. Diagonal runs use both axes at once, with the same total travel speed as straight runs. Each axis reverses independently at its boundary. Vertical roaming needs at least 18 logical units of spare height beyond Hood's footprint (one second of travel); shallower areas keep roaming horizontal. Tall areas can support vertical roaming even without spare width. Hood reverses at the boundaries and keeps the same gentle speed and rest periods.
